# 06 Incident response

5 requirements. When it goes wrong, you act instead of improvising.

What a first assessment tends to find: A plan nobody has walked through, or no plan, and contract reporting timelines nobody has read.

The work: Names, phone numbers, a definition of an incident involving Specified Information, containment steps for your actual environment, and the contract's reporting obligations with their timelines. Reporting obligations and their clocks live in your contract clauses and differ between contracts, so read yours rather than a summary. Then exercise it once a year on a tabletop, because a plan nobody has walked through is a document, and the difference shows within 10 minutes of a real event.

## 03.06.01 Incident handling

Have a working incident-handling capability that covers preparation, detection, analysis, containment, eradication, and recovery, consistent with your incident response plan.

Evidence to keep: incident handling procedure, roles, tooling that supports each phase.

[hans.study reading](https://hans.study/cpcsc/requirements/incident-response/#03.06.01) · [ITSP.10.171 text](https://www.cyber.gc.ca/en/guidance/protecting-specified-information-non-government-canada-systems-and-organizations-itsp10171)

- [ ] Met, with evidence filed

## 03.06.02 Incident monitoring, reporting, and response assistance

Track and document incidents, report suspected ones internally within your time limit, report to the authorities you name, and provide help for users who need to report.

Evidence to keep: incident register, reporting contacts and time limits, support contact.

You set: time period, authorities. Record each value in your system security plan.

[hans.study reading](https://hans.study/cpcsc/requirements/incident-response/#03.06.02) · [ITSP.10.171 text](https://www.cyber.gc.ca/en/guidance/protecting-specified-information-non-government-canada-systems-and-organizations-itsp10171)

- [ ] Met, with evidence filed

## 03.06.03 Incident response testing

Test the incident response capability on a schedule. A tabletop exercise counts if it's recorded.

Evidence to keep: exercise record with date, participants, and findings.

You set: frequency. Record each value in your system security plan.

[hans.study reading](https://hans.study/cpcsc/requirements/incident-response/#03.06.03) · [ITSP.10.171 text](https://www.cyber.gc.ca/en/guidance/protecting-specified-information-non-government-canada-systems-and-organizations-itsp10171)

- [ ] Met, with evidence filed

## 03.06.04 Incident response training

Train people in their incident response roles when they take them on, when systems change, and on a schedule, and keep the content current.

Evidence to keep: training records for incident roles, training content.

You set: time period, frequency, events. Record each value in your system security plan.

[hans.study reading](https://hans.study/cpcsc/requirements/incident-response/#03.06.04) · [ITSP.10.171 text](https://www.cyber.gc.ca/en/guidance/protecting-specified-information-non-government-canada-systems-and-organizations-itsp10171)

- [ ] Met, with evidence filed

## 03.06.05 Incident response plan

Write an incident response plan that gives a roadmap, describes the structure, defines reportable incidents, covers information sharing, names responsible people, and gets reviewed and updated.

Evidence to keep: the plan, approved and dated, revision history, distribution list.
