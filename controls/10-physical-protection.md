# 10 Physical protection

5 requirements, 2 at Level 1. The hardware holding protected data sits behind physical access control with a short key list.

What a first assessment tends to find: The server closet is also the janitor's closet.

The work: A lock with a short key list, a visitor log and escort rule, cameras on entries and main routes, and held-open alarms on the enclave door.

## 03.10.01 Physical access authorizations (Level 1)

Keep an approved list of people with access to the physical location, issue credentials, review the list on a schedule, and remove people who no longer need access.

Evidence to keep: approved access list, review dates, removals recorded.

You set: frequency. Record each value in your system security plan.

## 03.10.02 Monitoring physical access

Monitor physical access to the facility to detect and respond to incidents, and review access logs on a schedule and after relevant events.

Evidence to keep: access logs, camera or alarm evidence, log review records.

You set: frequency, events or potential indications of events. Record each value in your system security plan.

## 03.10.06 Alternate work site

Decide which alternate work sites are allowed, such as home offices, and apply defined security requirements there.

Evidence to keep: remote work policy, site requirements, approvals.

You set: security requirements. Record each value in your system security plan.

## 03.10.07 Physical access control (Level 1)

Verify physical access authorization at entry and exit points, control access with systems, devices, or guards, keep audit logs, and escort visitors and control what they do.

Evidence to keep: door control records, visitor log, key and card control, output device controls.

[hans.study reading](https://hans.study/cpcsc/requirements/physical-protection/#03.10.07) · [ITSP.10.171 text](https://www.cyber.gc.ca/en/guidance/protecting-specified-information-non-government-canada-systems-and-organizations-itsp10171)

## 03.10.08 Access control for transmission

Control physical access to distribution and transmission lines, such as network cabling and patch panels, inside your facilities.

Evidence to keep: locked comms rooms or cabinets, cabling inspection, access list.
