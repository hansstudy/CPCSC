# 02 Awareness and training

2 requirements. People who touch the data know what it is, how to handle it, and what an attack on them looks like this year.

What a first assessment tends to find: One onboarding video from 3 years ago, no record of who watched it, and a sales team posting shop-floor photos.

The work: Annual training with a sign-off sheet as the floor, role-specific content for admins and privileged users, and 5 minutes at a toolbox talk each quarter, which does more than the video anyone can click through.

## 03.02.01 Literacy training and awareness

Train every user at onboarding and on a schedule you set, and again when systems change. Cover insider threat, social engineering, and social mining, and keep the training content current.

Evidence to keep: training content, attendance records, schedule and the frequency you chose.

You set: frequency, events. Record each value in your system security plan.

## 03.02.02 Role-based training

People in security-relevant roles get training for those roles before they start the work, and again on a schedule. Administrators, the person who runs backups, and whoever handles incidents are typical examples.

Evidence to keep: role list, role-based training records, training content.

You set: frequency, events. Record each value in your system security plan.
